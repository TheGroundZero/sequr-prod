Adding AI attribution to my blog articles

DezeStijn • Sep 11, 2026 • 5 mins read
  • General

Intro

As the use of AI to touch up blog articles is now commonplace, I’ve decided to add AI attibution on articles where I used it.

You may have already see the “disclaimer” in my previous post on Volkswagen adding fake/lazy security to their app, resulting in GrapheneOS (and other FOSS) users no longer being able to install their app.

Even though I’m still somewhat of a skeptic when it comes to AI, there’s no denying that the technology is here to stay. The genie is out of the bottle, the math is know, the incentives are there. And to be honest: it can be tempting to use AI to touch up your text, or to help ideate a new article.

AI Content Disclosure Header

Looking for a standard method to communicate the usage of AI for a website, I stumbled upon an IETF draft for an AI Content Disclosure Header.

This draft proposed the introduction of a new HTTP header named AI-Disclosure with a few fields, the most important one being mode which describes how AI was used.

AI-Disclosure: mode=ai-originated;
               model="gpt-4";
               provider="OpenAI";
               reviewed-by="editorial-team";
               date=@1745286896

Only the mode field is mandatory, the rest provide optional, additional context about the AI model used, the provider, human review, or the generation timestamp, respectively.

Mode ValueDescription
noneIndicates that AI was not used in the creation or substantive modification of the content.
ai-modifiedIndicates AI was used to assist with or modify content primarily created by humans. The source material was not AI-generated. Examples include AI-based grammar checking, style suggestions, or generating highlights or summaries of human-written text.
ai-originatedIndicates the core content was initially generated by AI but subsequently reviewed, edited, or significantly guided by humans. This suggests human oversight for accuracy or appropriateness, even if the originality for copyright purposes might be affected.
machine-generatedIndicates the content was primarily or entirely generated by AI with minimal or no human intervention or review post-generation. AI may be responsible for substantive assertions or conclusions.

Hugo implementation

I’ve implemented this AI disclosure/attribution in 2 ways: replicating the actual AI-Disclosure HTTP header as a meta tag and including a footer to relevant blog posts. These changes are now part of my poison theme.

Frontmatter

I’ve basically added each of the fields from the draft as a field in the frontmatter.

The frontmatter for my Volkswagen post looks as follows:

ai-disclosure:
  mode: ai-originated
  model: "GPT-5.6 Luna"
  provider: "OpenAI"
  reviewed-by: "site-owner"
  message: "This article was ideated with AI and further adapter by a human"

Meta tag

As I can’t modify HTTP headers from Hugo, I’ve implemented is as a meta tag instead.

{{ with index .Params "ai-disclosure" }}
<meta name="AI-Disclosure"
  mode="{{ .mode }}"
  {{- with .model }} model="{{ . }}"{{ end }}
  {{- with .provider }} provider="{{ . }}"{{ end }}
  {{- with (index . "reviewed-by") }} reviewed-by="{{ . }}"{{ else }} reviewed-by="none"{{ end }}
  {{- with .date }} date="{{ . }}"{{ end }}
/>
{{- end }}

This partial should be fairly easy to understand:

  • First of all, we check whether an ai-disclosure parameter was provided in the frontmatter.
  • As the mode is considered mandatory, I’m not checking whether that keys is provided. Not providing it will result in a build error.
  • While reviewed-by isn’t mandatory, I do consider it to be a valuable indicator. If no value is provided, it will default to none.
  • The other fields are only added if they are provided.

Article footer

Since my blog is aimed at human readers first, I’m also adding a human-readable disclosure at the bottom of blog posts as well.

{{ with index .Params "ai-disclosure" }}
<span class="ai-attribution" data-ai-attribution-mode="{{ .mode }}" data-ai-attribution-reviewed-by="{{ with (index . "reviewed-by") }}{{ . }}{{ else }}none{{ end }}">
  <span class="ai-attribution__icon" aria-hidden="true">
    <span class="ai-attribution__icon--light">
      {{- partial "svg" "AI_black" -}}
    </span>
    <span class="ai-attribution__icon--dark">
      {{- partial "svg" "AI_white" -}}
    </span>
  </span>
  <span class="ai-attribution__text">
    {{- with .message -}}
      {{ . }}
    {{- else -}}
      {{- if eq .mode "ai-modified" -}}
        AI was used to assist with or modify content primarily created by humans.
      {{- else if eq .mode "ai-originated" -}}
        This article's core content was initially generated by AI but subsequently reviewed, edited, or significantly guided by humans.
      {{- else if eq .mode "machine-generated" -}}
        This article's content was primarily or entirely generated by AI with minimal or no human intervention or review post-generation.
      {{- else -}}
        This article has (partially) been written by AI.
      {{- end -}}
    {{- end -}}
  </span>
</span>
{{ end }}

This one works in a similar way:

  • The disclosure is only added if ai-disclosure is defined in the frontmatter.
  • We add the mode and reviewed-by as data-* attributes.
  • The disclaimer consists out of an AI logo developed by the European Commission and a description.
    We’re doing a bit extra here to support a light and dark mode.
  • The description uses some hardcoded values mapped to the different modes described earlier.
    But we’re also leaving room for a custom message provided in the frontmatter as well.

In the end that results in the footer shown earler and this meta tag:

<meta name="AI-Disclosure" mode="ai-originated" model="GPT-5.6 Luna" provider="OpenAI" reviewed-by="site-owner">
